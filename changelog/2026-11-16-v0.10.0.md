**iota now runs full screen**, the way `vim` or `less` do: the conversation
fills the window and the input box stays at the bottom. Resizing no longer
leaves blank or doubled rows — the conversation is laid out again for the new
width, tables and code blocks included. **The mouse works in the chat**: the
wheel scrolls, a click places the cursor, and dragging selects and copies.
The price is a change of habit: **while iota runs, your terminal's own
scrollback, Cmd+F and tmux copy mode no longer reach the conversation** —
`Ctrl+O` writes it into the terminal's history, `Ctrl+R` searches it, and new
keys scroll it.

## Upgrading: the terminal's scrollback and the new keys

What used to be the terminal's job is now iota's while it runs:

| To | Before | Now |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Scroll back | the terminal's scrollbar | the wheel, **PgUp / PgDn** (Fn+↑/↓ on a MacBook) |
| Jump to the top / back to the latest | — | **Ctrl+Home / Ctrl+End** |
| Search the conversation | Cmd+F, tmux copy mode | **Ctrl+R**, or `/` while scrolled back with an empty input |
| Select across screens | the terminal's selection | drag in iota; or **Ctrl+O** first, then the terminal's |
| The terminal's own selection | plain drag | **Shift-drag** (Option-drag in iTerm2) |

- **Ctrl+O** writes the conversation into the terminal's history, so its
  scrollback, search and selection reach all of it; press any key to come
  back. Each line is written only once, however often you press it.
- **When you quit** — Ctrl+D, `/exit`, Ctrl+C at the prompt, or a `kill` —
  the conversation is written into the terminal's history, as before. After
  a crash or `kill -9` it is not; `iota resume` brings it back.
- **A resumed session shows its last three rounds.** Scrolling up does not
  reach older ones; `/export` has the whole conversation. This holds for
  `/resume`, `-c`, `iota resume` and a bot.
- **Ctrl+F is not search**: it still moves the cursor one character right.
- **If you would rather keep the terminal's mouse**, write `mouse: false` in
  `~/.iota.yaml`: iota leaves the mouse alone, and the wheel and selection are
  the terminal's own (for more than one screen, press Ctrl+O first).

## Added

- **The mouse works in the chat.** The wheel scrolls the conversation —
  scrolling up stops following new output, back at the bottom it follows
  again. A click puts the input's cursor where you clicked. In a dialog a
  click moves to a row and a second click chooses it, so one stray click
  never answers an approval. Drag to select, double-click a word,
  triple-click a line; letting go copies. Hold the drag at the top or bottom
  edge and the conversation keeps scrolling, so a selection can span any
  number of screens. The copy goes to your clipboard (`pbcopy`, `wl-copy` or
  `xclip`), to tmux's paste buffer inside tmux, and over SSH to the terminal
  on your side. It is plain text, and a long line wrapped on screen is copied
  as the one line it is. The status row says what was copied.
- **herdr-core (`hc`) sees iota's state.** In a terminal of herdr-core, the
  terminal behind herdr's desktop app, `hc get` and `hc wait` see iota as
  `working`, `blocked` at an approval or another dialog, `idle`, or `error`
  after a failed turn, and `hc send` refuses to type into an open approval.
  A `-m` run reports too. Inside tmux in an hc terminal, iota keeps the
  terminal's progress bar instead. The herdr multiplexer is unchanged.

## Changed

- **iota takes the whole terminal window.** The old inline chat is gone, and
  so is the cost of resizing it: no blank or doubled rows after a drag, no
  input box a few columns short while you drag. iota also starts on a
  terminal that never answers its startup checks, though there the first key
  you type is lost.
- **New keys.** Besides scrolling and search (above): scrolled back, new
  output does not pull you down, and the bottom line counts the new lines
  below. In a search, matches are highlighted and counted, `n`/`p` step
  through them, Enter stays where you are and Esc goes back. **Ctrl+Z**
  suspends iota and gives the terminal back to the shell; `fg` brings it
  back, redrawn at the window's size.
- **Resizing lays the conversation out again.** Wider or narrower, what is on
  screen is laid out for the new width instead of being cut and wrapped: a
  table's columns are recomputed, a list's wrapped lines stay under the
  item, a code block wraps under its indent, a quote keeps its bar on every
  row, a diff fills the new width, and paragraphs re-wrap both ways. Long
  list items and code lines now wrap neatly at any width.
- **Code blocks, tables, lists and quotes appear line by line** while the
  reply is written, at the window's width, instead of behind a
  `rendering… · N lines` counter. A display formula shows as its source until
  its closing `$$`, then as the formula.
- **A dialog never takes the whole window.** A dialog's list, `/status`,
  `/file`'s browser and the slash-completion list take at most half the
  window and never more than 15 rows; a longer list scrolls inside it
  (`↑`/`↓` a row, `←`/`→` a page, `g`/`G` top and bottom). Closing it gives
  the conversation back where it was.
- **Slash completion is a list.** Typing `/` opens a list above the input,
  one command per row with its description. `↑`/`↓` move, Enter runs the
  highlighted command, Esc puts back what you typed, Tab completes as before,
  and a click highlights an item. `/skills ` lists each skill with its own
  description.
- **Your messages stand out behind a gray `▌`** down their left edge, a
  resumed session's included; with `NO_COLOR` the `▌` alone marks them. The
  input box has no `❯` any more: what you type starts at the left edge and
  wraps at the full width.
- **The two rules around the input box are solid again** (`─` instead of the
  faint dashed `┄`).
